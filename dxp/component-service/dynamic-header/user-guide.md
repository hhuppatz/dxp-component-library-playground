# User Guide: Editing the Dynamic Header Component in Squiz Matrix

Welcome to the user guide for the **Dynamic Header** component in Squiz Matrix. This guide will walk you through everything you need to know about editing and customizing a Dynamic Header so that it looks great and communicates effectively on your page. No technical skills are required—just follow the steps below!

---

## What is the Dynamic Header Component?

The **Dynamic Header** is a handy web component that allows you to add headers and supporting text to your website. It consists of:
- A **title** (the main heading).
- An optional block of **supporting content** that appears directly under the title.

### Where is the Dynamic Header Used?  
This component is perfect for introducing new sections on a page or drawing attention to important content. It’s customizable, easy to edit, and helps improve your website’s structure and readability.

---

## What Does the Dynamic Header Look Like?

Here’s what the Dynamic Header might look like on your page:  

```
[Main Heading (H2 or other level)]  
Supporting text goes here. You can add more content or leave it blank.
```

By default:
- The title is displayed as an H2 heading (`<h2>`), but you can change it to any heading level (H1 to H6).
- You can include additional text below the title if needed.

---

## Step-by-Step Instructions for Editing the Component

Follow these steps to customize your Dynamic Header:

### 1. **Title (Required)**  
This is the heading that will grab the reader's attention.

   1. Click on the **Title** field.
   2. Enter the desired heading text (e.g., “Welcome to Our Services”).
   3. Keep your title concise and impactful—avoid long sentences.

   > **Tip**: A clear and strong title helps users understand the section at a glance.

   **Default Value:** *Section title*

---

### 2. **Title Level (Optional)**  
Choose how large or prominent the title should appear by selecting the appropriate heading level (H1 to H6):
- H1 is the largest and boldest.
- H6 is the smallest and most subtle.

   1. Locate the **Title Level** dropdown menu.
   2. Select a heading level from the list (`h1`, `h2`, `h3`, `h4`, `h5`, or `h6`).
   3. Use heading levels consistently (e.g., avoid using `h1` multiple times on the same page unless they're top-level).

   **Default Value:** *H2*

   > **Tip**: Use H1 for the main page heading, and H2-H6 for subheadings, depending on the importance of the content.

---

### 3. **Content (Optional)**  
This is the supporting text that appears below the title.

   1. Click on the **Content** field.
   2. Add text to provide additional context (e.g., “Explore a range of services tailored to your needs”).
   3. Keep this text concise but informative.
   4. Use proper formatting (e.g., bullet points, bolding) to make the text easy to read.

   > **Tip**: Adding relevant keywords here can help with search engine optimization (SEO).

---

## Required vs Optional Fields

- **Required Field**:  
   - **Title**: Every Dynamic Header must have a title.

- **Optional Fields**:
   - **Title Level**: You can leave this as the default (H2) or choose another level.
   - **Content**: You can omit supporting content if your title is self-explanatory.

---

## Conditional Fields

There are no strict conditional fields for this component. All options are available and visible for you to customize, regardless of your choices in other fields.

---

## Best Practices for Using the Dynamic Header

Follow these tips to get the best results with your Dynamic Header:

1. **Stay Consistent:**  
   Stick to the heading levels defined in your website's design style guide. For instance, if H2 is used for section headings, avoid switching to H3 or H4 unless you are creating subheadings.

2. **Keep Text Clear and Concise:**  
   - Use short, strong titles that clearly communicate the purpose of your section.
   - Supporting text should be easy to read and directly relevant to the title.

3. **Accessibility Matters:**  
   - Use heading levels hierarchically (e.g., don’t use an H3 without having a preceding H2).
   - Write descriptive titles to help visitors and screen readers understand the content.

4. **Preview Your Work:**  
   Always preview the page to see how the Dynamic Header looks alongside the rest of your content.

---

## Quick Recap

Here’s what you need to know to effectively use the Dynamic Header:
- **Title** is required—make it clear and concise.
- **Title Level** is optional but allows you to control how prominent the title appears. Use it wisely to maintain a logical structure.
- **Content** is optional, but it’s great for adding supporting details.

By following these steps and tips, you’ll create Dynamic Headers that look polished and professional, helping your content shine.

Happy editing! 🎉
